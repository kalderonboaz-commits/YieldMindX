  measurement. No connection to any of the 9 planted relationships'
  named parameters. Given the leakage family's known dominance (#6), this
  could be a genuine additional interaction involving that family, or a
  standalone synthetic-generator structure not covered by the 9-item
  reference table. Cannot be classified as spurious purely from evidence

- Cross-stage findings beyond Gm_max and Drain_Lag_1: 58 STRONG cross-
  stage pairs were found in total, spanning many different measurement
  families (capacitance, threshold voltage, resistance, thickness
  measurements across ANODE/THIN/TFRP/BRGM/SIN/TOPSIN modules). This is a
  SYSTEMATIC, family-wide pattern, not an isolated finding. Two
  competing, currently-indistinguishable explanations: (a) the synthetic
  generator plants a broader "stage-to-stage consistency matters"
  principle beyond the single documented Gm_max example, or (b) many
  columns across modules are correlated noisy proxies of a small number
  of true latent yield drivers, so almost ANY two-stage pair sharing
  exposure to those drivers would show a spurious "cross-stage difference
  correlates with yield" pattern by construction, independent of any
  genuine "consistency effect." This cannot be resolved from the
  available evidence and is flagged as genuinely uncertain rather than
  asserted either way.

- The TOPSIN_ET_DC_PIV_Drain_Lag_1 "hub" pattern in 3-way findings (common
  member in the large majority of 112 STRONG triples): most plausibly
  reflects DUPLICATED/RELATED MEASUREMENT-FAMILY EFFECTS -- i.e. this one

  many statistically real but partially redundant 3-way patterns with
  different third variables, rather than 112 independent 3-way phenomena.
  Should be read as strong evidence for ONE robust conditional effect
  involving this parameter, not 112 separate discoveries.

FINAL INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Did v2 materially improve recovery of the planted relationships?
   Mixed, not uniform. Practical recovery rate improved materially
   (22.2% -> 33.3%), driven almost entirely by ONE clean win (#8). Strict
   recovery rate is UNCHANGED (11.1% both). Two relationships genuinely
   REGRESSED (#2, #6) -- this is reported honestly rather than only
   highlighting the improvement.

2. Which relationship classes improved most?
   Cross-stage / derived-delta relationships: the ENTIRE point of the new
   cross-stage module, and it delivered exactly the intended result (#8,
   NOT TESTABLE -> EXACTLY FOUND, STRONG, 5/5-fold-stable, correct
   direction). Previously-zero-LightGBM-importance nonlinear single-
   parameter relationships also improved partially (#1, MISSED ->
   PARTIALLY FOUND).

3. Which planted relationships are still missed?
   #2 (PAE benefit) and #7 (Alignment process window) -- both show zero
   stable evidence under v2's model-free method, in addition to being
   incompletely characterized under v1's LightGBM-based method.

4. Are any misses now true model/method failures rather than coverage
   failures?
   YES, and this is the most important methodological finding of this
   checkpoint. #7 (Alignment) had real (if modest) LightGBM importance in
   v1 but fell outside the screening threshold -- a coverage failure. In
   v2, with 100% coverage and a completely different, model-free
   detection method, it STILL shows zero stable evidence. Two
   methodologically distinct approaches now agree in failing on this
   relationship -- meaningfully stronger evidence of a genuine detection
   limitation (plausibly the true relationship's narrow functional form)
   rather than a fixable coverage gap. The same logic applies to #2 (PAE).
   By contrast, #4, #5, and #9 remain COVERAGE failures, not method
   failures -- verified directly: the relevant parameters/pairs never
   reached the confirmable stage of the pipeline, so no method-capability
   conclusion can be drawn about them yet.

5. Is LightGBM + the improved discovery layer now sufficient for Stage 2,
   or is there still a clear reason to test a second model?
   There is a clear, evidence-based (not reflexive) reason to consider a
   different detection approach for a SPECIFIC, narrow class of
   relationships: #2 and #7 now show consistent, method-independent
   non-detection (tree-based SHAP/PDP AND model-free quantile-binning
   both fail). This narrow, specific failure pattern -- not general
   dissatisfaction with LightGBM -- is the actual evidence basis for
   considering a smoother/parametric detection method (e.g. spline-based
   GAM fitting, or a differently-parameterized shape test) for these two
   relationship types specifically. This would not necessarily require a
   second full model for Stage 2 prediction/discovery generally -- it is
   a targeted method question for a narrow, well-identified gap, not a
   broad case for replacing LightGBM. Separately, #4, #5, and #9 remain
   candidate-GENERATION coverage gaps that a different model would not
   automatically fix without also changing the candidate-selection
   strategy (e.g. a less lower-order-dependent 3-way/2-way candidate
   generation approach). No further model changes are made in this
   checkpoint pending explicit approval.
