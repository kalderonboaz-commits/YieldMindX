------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PAIR SCORING FORMULAS (exact breakdown)
------------------------------------------------------------------
score_1 (compute_pairwise_screen_scores, src/explain/stage2_v2_pair_screen.py):
  For feature i: G_i = per-row quantile-bin MEAN of y (6 bins, marginal
  fit). C_i = per-row CENTERED BIN INDEX of i (a linear/rank-like basis).
  Resid_i = y_centered - G_i (removes i's OWN marginal effect only).
  score(i,j) = max(|corr(Resid_i, C_j)|, |corr(Resid_j, C_i)|).
  Sensitive to: association between i's residual and a LINEAR/MONOTONIC
  transform of j's rank. Structurally a RANK-CORRELATION test, not a
  general non-additivity test.

score_2 (compute_nonadditivity_screen, src/explain/stage2_v3_common.py):
  4x4 quantile-binned grid per pair. ADDITIVE[q1,q2] = marginal_mean_i[q1]
  + marginal_mean_j[q2] (unconditional, UNSHRUNK). JOINT[q1,q2] = raw
  observed cell mean (also UNSHRUNK). score = count-weighted mean of
  (JOINT-ADDITIVE)^2 over 16 cells.
  Sensitive to: genuine departure of the observed joint surface from the
  additive sum, in ANY shape (monotonic or not) -- the theoretically
  correct test family, same spirit as the validator's own confirmatory
  test.

------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TRIPLE CANDIDATE SCORING
------------------------------------------------------------------
Triple generation does NOT use score_1/score_2 at all. It is built
entirely from (a) the VALIDATOR's own confirmed 2-way edge strength
(interaction_strength_delta_r2, per-node top-k subgraph), (b) LightGBM
tree-path co-occurrence counts, (c) a linear residual-vs-raw-value
correlation (channel 4 -- same structural weakness as score_1). Triple
coverage is therefore entirely DOWNSTREAM of pair-screening coverage: any
weakness in score_1/score_2 propagates directly into which triples can
ever be reached.

------------------------------------------------------------------
3. BIAS ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------
score_1: YES, biased toward the PARTNER'S OWN marginal effect. Resid_i
  only removes i's marginal fit -- if j independently predicts y, Resid_i
  will correlate with j's rank simply BECAUSE j has main-effect signal,
  not because i and j interact. This conflates "j is individually
  important" with "i and j interact."
score_2: not biased toward marginal effects by formula (marginals are
  explicitly subtracted before squaring), but IS indirectly biased toward
  globally-important/high-variance features via SCALE (no normalization
  by total variance) and toward INDEPENDENT (uncorrelated) pairs, since
  correlated pairs produce sparser, noisier 2D cells (common in this
  dataset's redundant feature families) -- exactly where a real but
  moderate interaction is most likely to be under-detected.

------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW A REAL INTERACTION CAN SCORE LOW HERE BUT PASS THE VALIDATOR
------------------------------------------------------------------
(i) score_1 misses conditional effects that are NON-MONOTONIC in the
    partner (e.g. strong at both tails, weak mid-range) -- a linear rank
    correlation nets near zero even though the effect is real.
(ii) score_1's marginal-effect contamination can partially cancel a real
    interaction signal in the aggregate correlation.
(iii) score_2's coarse (4x4=16-cell), UNSHRUNK cell means are noisy
    estimates, especially for correlated pairs with unevenly populated
    cells -- sampling noise can push a real, moderate effect below any
    cutoff (global OR per-feature-local).
(iv) The validator's OWN confirmatory test (fit_additive_joint) uses
    shrinkage (toward the additive prediction, weighted by cell count)
    and 5-fold cross-validated averaging -- both explicitly designed to
    suppress exactly this kind of small-sample noise. The broad-screen
    score has NEITHER protection, creating a real asymmetry: the
    confirmatory test is more sensitive/stable than the screen that
    decides what reaches it.
Empirical corroboration: the two remaining uncovered pairs from the prior
checkpoint ranked in the low-hundreds-to-low-thousands (of ~1,404) on
BOTH score_1 and score_2, for BOTH member features -- consistent with a
combination of (i)-(iv), not an isolated one-off.

VERDICT: METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATION, not a bug. Every formula computes
exactly what it is designed to compute, correctly. The limitation is a
DESIGN trade-off (cheap, fully-vectorizable, exhaustive-coverage proxies
necessarily sacrifice the shrinkage/CV robustness the confirmatory test
can afford per-candidate) -- disclosed, reasonable, but real.

------------------------------------------------------------------
5-6. ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED
------------------------------------------------------------------
(A) Add SHRINKAGE to score_2 (mirror fit_additive_joint's proven cell-
    count-weighted regularization toward the additive prediction).
    Coverage benefit: directly targets the diagnosed noise mechanism
    (iii/iv), especially for correlated/redundant pairs.
    Cost: negligible -- same vectorized matmul structure, one extra
    elementwise blend.
    False-positive risk: reduced, if anything (shrinkage is conservative).
    Compatibility: pair_screen.py only; validator untouched.
    Ground-truth leakage: none -- shrinkage constant is a generic,
    disclosed value, not fit to any known answer.

(B) Generalize score_1 to a binned (non-monotonic) residual-dependence
    statistic (eta-squared / variance-explained across the partner's
    bins, replacing linear correlation).
    Coverage benefit: fixes score_1's structural non-monotonicity blind
    spot -- but score_2 already covers non-monotonic JOINT departure by
    construction, making this largely redundant with (A) rather than
    additive to it.
    Cost: moderate -- new vectorized machinery, more code than (A).
    Compatibility: pair_screen.py only.
    Ground-truth leakage: none.

(C) Scale-normalize score_2 (Friedman-H-style: divide by total joint
    variance instead of using raw squared departure).
    Coverage benefit: removes the high-variance-feature scale bias.
    Cost: negligible, one extra division.
    Compatibility/leakage: same as (A) -- a natural, cheap companion.

(D) Hierarchical residual re-screening (screen residuals after removing
    all confirmed single-parameter effects).
    Coverage benefit: potentially high but unproven.
    Cost: highest of the options -- multi-pass, changes what "y" means
    at screening time.
    Compatibility: larger structural change.
    Verdict: reasonable future direction, not the smallest fix.

------------------------------------------------------------------
7. RECOMMENDATION
------------------------------------------------------------------
(A) Add shrinkage to score_2, optionally paired with (C)'s cheap scale
normalization. Smallest change, directly targets the empirically-
diagnosed noise mechanism, reuses a method ALREADY proven and audited in
the confirmatory validator (methodological consistency), stays fully
vectorized/exhaustive, and requires no new score family.
