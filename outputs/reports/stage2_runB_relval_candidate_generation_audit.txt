1. CURRENT CANDIDATE-GENERATION CHANNELS
==================================================================

PAIR SCREEN (src/explain/stage2_relval_pair_screen.py) -- 7 channels,
union promoted = 6,677 / 986,310 pairs (0.68%):

  A/B: global top-2,000 by raw score_1 / score_2 (fresh, vectorized,
       100%-coverage broad scores). Evidence: marginal-residual proxy /
       2D non-additivity energy. Filter: a SINGLE GLOBAL rank cutoff
       across all 986,310 pairs. Bias: strongly favors feature FAMILIES
       whose typical score magnitude is large (e.g. leakage/capacitance
       columns dominate observed top scores) -- a real but modest-
       magnitude pair from another family is suppressed by nothing
       feature-specific, purely by shared competition with unrelated
       high-magnitude pairs.
  C: both members v4 HIGH_EVIDENCE, then global top-2,000 of that
     C(364,2)=66,066-pair sub-universe by score_2. Filter: a SECOND
     global rank cutoff, now WITHIN an already-restricted eligible set.
     A HIGH-tier feature's own single best partner can still lose to
     OTHER HIGH x HIGH pairs across the whole 66k sub-universe.
  D: pair already v5-confirmed STRONG/MODERATE, global top-2,000 by v5's
     OWN incremental R^2. Same global-competition structure, on top of
     v5's own prior candidate-generation limits.
  E/F: reused frozen v3 shortlist / v2 cross-stage evidence, used in
       full (no additional cutoff -- small, 600/155 pairs).
  G: both spline-nonlinear, global top-1,000 of 217,470 eligible.

  Correlated/proxy suppression: YES -- channels A/B/C/D/G are ALL global
  rankings over a shared pool. A feature with several near-duplicate
  correlated siblings (common in this dataset) has its "signal budget"
  split across siblings, and if a rival, unrelated family's pairs simply
  score higher in raw magnitude, NONE of a feature's true partners need
  individually outscore anything -- they just need to collectively lose
  the shared top-K race.
  Bias toward globally important features: YES for channels C/D (require
  HIGH-tier or prior-v5-confirmed membership) -- a feature that is
  individually real but only MEDIUM-tier is invisible to 3 of 7 channels
  entirely, and can only be reached via the raw, also-globally-competed,
  channels A/B/G.

TRIPLE SCREEN (src/explain/stage2_relval_triple_screen.py) -- 5 channels,
13,064 / ~461.26M possible triples (0.0028%):

  Edge subgraph (feeds channels 1+2): global top-2,500 STRONG+MODERATE
  2-way edges by THIS engine's own delta_R^2 (out of ~6,329 confirmed
  edges). Same global-competition pattern as pair channels A-D, one level
  up: a feature's own strongest edge can be excluded from the subgraph
  entirely if the edge's raw effect size is modest relative to the
  overall STRONG-edge population, even though the edge is itself real and
  FDR-confirmed.
  Channel 2 (shared-node chaining) correctly does NOT require the third
  pairwise edge to exist (the generic v3-derived fix) -- but only
  operates on nodes that already made it INTO the capped subgraph above,
  so the fix cannot help a node excluded at the subgraph-selection step.
  Channel 2's own CHAIN_CAP=4,000 overflow handling uses a RANDOM
  subsample (not evidence-ranked) when raw chains exceed the cap -- an
  additional, non-generalizable source of coverage loss once triggered.
  Channels 3-5 (tree co-occurrence, residual-conditional, v5
  corroboration) are all seeded from the SAME capped subgraph or from a
  small top-15 STRONG-pair list -- they inherit, rather than fix, the
  subgraph's exclusions.

==================================================================
2. ROOT-CAUSE DIAGNOSIS
==================================================================
Case-study check (frozen v4 evidence graph + pair-screen coverage file,
read-only, no new computation):
  - Two features individually confirmed, evidence_tier=MEDIUM_EVIDENCE
    each: invisible to channels C/D/G by construction (require HIGH-tier
    or prior-confirmed membership); only reachable via the global-
    competition channels A/B, where they did not clear the top-2,000 of
    986,310.
  - Two features individually confirmed, evidence_tier=HIGH_EVIDENCE
    each (one with 8 independent supporting methods): BOTH are promoted
    via channel C/D, but each with a DIFFERENT partner -- their specific
    pairing with EACH OTHER never wins either channel's shared top-2,000
    rank race.

Generalizable conclusion: the bottleneck is NOT ranking-signal quality,
pruning aggressiveness in isolation, correlation clustering, or
computational limits -- every channel already covers its input space
exhaustively (100% for pairs' broad scores; systematic, disclosed
sampling for triples). The bottleneck is that every promotion channel
uses a SINGLE, SHARED, GLOBALLY-COMPETED top-K cutoff. A feature that is
individually real and evidenced has NO channel that guarantees it a
"personal" chance to be tested against its own best partner(s) --
coverage is entirely an emergent side-effect of how that feature's best
pairing compares to every OTHER candidate pairing in the same shared
pool, dominated by whichever feature families happen to produce the
largest raw score magnitudes. This affects both pair generation (2000-
row global top-K, four separate times) and triple generation (2500-edge
global subgraph cutoff feeding every triple channel).

==================================================================
3-4. CANDIDATE STRATEGIES CONSIDERED
==================================================================

(a) PER-FEATURE / PER-NODE GUARANTEED LOCAL TOP-K (recommended)
    Idea: for every feature with independent single-parameter evidence
    (CONFIRMED in the frozen single-parameter validator output -- already
    computed, ground-truth-free, generic), guarantee it k candidate pairs
    drawn from ITS OWN row of the already-computed score_1/score_2
    matrices (a per-feature, non-competed top-k), not a shared pool.
    Same principle for triples: select the confirmed-edge subgraph via a
    per-node top-k of each node's own best edges, not one global cutoff.
    - Coverage benefit: directly closes the diagnosed root cause for
      BOTH observed failure modes (tier-gated exclusion and cross-family
      crowding-out).
    - Cost: near-zero extra compute -- score matrices are already fully
      computed; a per-row top-k is a cheap sort over each feature's own
      ~1,404 values, done once.
    - False-positive risk: unchanged -- these candidates still go through
      the SAME unmodified confirmatory test + permutation FDR gate; a
      per-feature guarantee only affects which pairs get a CHANCE to be
      tested, not whether they pass.
    - Compatibility: touches only pair_screen.py / triple_screen.py (new
      channel), zero changes to 2way.py/3way.py/common.py/fdr.py/
      negative_control.py.
    - Ground-truth leakage risk: none -- trigger set is "single-parameter
      CONFIRMED" (already-frozen, generic evidence), selection is by
      already-computed generic scores.

(b) RAISE THE GLOBAL TOP-K CAPS (e.g. 2,000 -> 10,000)
    - Coverage benefit: modest and non-principled -- delays but does not
      remove the crowding-out mechanism; dominant families still win a
      bigger race.
    - Cost: roughly linear increase in 2way.py confirmatory runtime
      (minutes, still cheap) but adds many more low-value hypotheses to
      the FDR family, diluting statistical power for everything else.
    - False-positive risk: negligible increase (FDR gate unaffected).
    - Compatibility: trivial one-line constant change.
    - Ground-truth leakage risk: none.
    - Verdict: cheap but doesn't fix the actual mechanism; not preferred.

(c) FEATURE-FAMILY / CORRELATION-CLUSTER-AWARE PAIRING
    Idea: bucket features by correlation cluster (reusing/adapting Stage
    1's clustering module) and guarantee within-cluster and cross-cluster
    candidate pairs.
    - Coverage benefit: potentially high, and directly addresses
      redundant-proxy suppression.
    - Cost: moderate -- requires adapting an existing but not currently
      Stage-2-wired clustering utility to the 1,405-feature Run B
      universe; more implementation surface than (a).
    - False-positive risk: unchanged (same downstream gate).
    - Compatibility: pair-generation-only change, compatible.
    - Ground-truth leakage risk: none.
    - Verdict: a good COMPLEMENTARY channel for a later checkpoint, but a
      larger, less surgical change than (a) for the same diagnosed root
      cause -- clusters would still need a WITHIN-cluster/cross-cluster
      selection rule, which reduces to the same "avoid a single global
      top-K" principle as (a), just organized differently.

(d) FIX THE RANDOM-SUBSAMPLE OVERFLOW IN TRIPLE CHAINING (CHAIN_CAP)
    - Coverage benefit: small, narrow (only matters when raw_chains >
      4,000) but a genuine, currently non-evidence-based information loss.
    - Cost: negligible (replace random.choice with an evidence-ranked
      top-K, e.g. by summed constituent-edge score).
    - False-positive risk: none.
    - Compatibility: trivial, isolated fix.
    - Ground-truth leakage risk: none.
    - Verdict: worth bundling as a small companion fix alongside (a), not
      a standalone strategy.

==================================================================
5. RECOMMENDATION
==================================================================
Adopt (a), PER-FEATURE/PER-NODE GUARANTEED LOCAL TOP-K, as a new,
additively-unioned channel in both pair_screen.py and triple_screen.py
(existing channels A-G / 1-5 remain unchanged -- this is purely additive,
zero risk to what already works). Also bundle in fix (d) (evidence-ranked
overflow selection instead of random subsampling in triple chaining) as a
one-line companion change, since it is the same class of issue.

Why (a) over (b)/(c): it is the smallest, most surgical change that
targets the EXACT diagnosed mechanism (global vs. per-feature ranking),
reuses data already fully computed (no new expensive scoring pass, no new
external module), and is the most directly generalizable across BOTH
pair and triple generation -- (b) doesn't fix the mechanism, and (c),
while a reasonable future complement, is a larger change that ultimately
still needs the same per-bucket "guarantee, don't just compete" principle
to work.
